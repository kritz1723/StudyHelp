Why the word, not the verse

English is the wrong unit of study. “Love” in one translation and “love” in another may stand for different Greek words — agape, phileo, eros, storge — and one Greek word may be turned into a dozen different English ones across the centuries. So an English search is only the door: it opens onto the words underneath, and everything is traced from there.

Where this is still incomplete. The Septuagint — the Greek Old Testament that carried Hebrew meaning into the Greek of the New Testament — is loaded and can be read, but it carries no word-by-word tagging that is free to use, so the chain from Hebrew through Greek cannot yet be followed word by word. Meanings currently come from one dictionary, so where scholars disagree, that disagreement cannot yet be shown. The deuterocanonical books can be read in the versions that carry them, but no freely licensed word-by-word tagging covers them, so choosing a wider canon changes what can be read, not yet what can be searched.
