Why the word, not the verse alone

English is the wrong unit of study. “Love” in one translation and “love” in another may stand for different Greek words — agape, phileo, eros, storge — and one Greek word may become a dozen different English ones across the centuries. So a verse is the door, and the words underneath are the room.

Where this is still incomplete. Word-by-word glosses are modern; the historical translations here are not aligned word to word, so meaning can be traced across languages but not yet across centuries. The Septuagint can be read but not searched. No freely licensed tagging covers the deuterocanonical books, so a wider canon changes what can be read, not what can be counted.
