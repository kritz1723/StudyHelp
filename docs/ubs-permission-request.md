# Draft: permission request to the United Bible Societies

StudyHelp cannot use the semantic-domain data it would most benefit from, because
that data is licensed to Clear Bible rather than to the public. Only the United
Bible Societies can change that, and only if asked. This is a draft for a human
to review, put on their own letterhead, and send — it is not sent automatically,
and nothing in the project depends on a reply.

**To:** the rights contact at the United Bible Societies (via semanticdictionary.org)
**Subject:** Request to use MARBLE and SDBH semantic-domain data in an open Bible study tool

---

Dear United Bible Societies,

I maintain StudyHelp, a free and open-source Bible word-study tool. It traces a
word from Hebrew, through the Greek of the Septuagint, into the Greek New
Testament and on into English, showing where a word first appears, everywhere it
is used, and what dictionaries say it has meant — attributing every claim to its
source and showing disagreement between authorities rather than resolving it.

The project uses only openly licensed or public-domain data. It draws on the
MACULA Greek and Hebrew datasets published by Biblica under CC BY 4.0, which
include semantic-domain and word-sense data from two of your works: the
Louw-Nida domains via the MARBLE project, and the Semantic Dictionary of Biblical
Hebrew edited by Reinier de Blois. Both are marked in MACULA as used with your
permission rather than openly licensed.

I have therefore not used them. The permission granted to Clear Bible is theirs,
not something a downstream project inherits, and taking the data on the strength
of its being technically present would misrepresent your terms.

I am writing to ask whether you would grant permission for these fields to be
used in StudyHelp, which is:

- non-commercial and free to use, with no advertising, subscription or paywall;
- open-source, with all code and data provenance public;
- rigorous about attribution — every claim on screen names the work it came from,
  and your works would be credited by name wherever their data appeared.

If a licence fee, a specific attribution wording, or a restriction on
redistribution would make this possible, I would welcome hearing the terms. If
the answer is no, that is entirely understood and the project will continue
without the data.

The reason for asking is straightforward. Semantic domains are the one thing that
would let this tool show how a word's range of meaning is actually structured,
rather than listing glosses. That is exactly what a reader trying to understand a
verse needs, and there is no comparable open dataset.

With thanks for your work, which has made a great deal of biblical scholarship
usable by people outside it.
